# Shared business approval capability

`governance.ts` remains the single approval engine. `approval-workflow.ts` handles draft editing, submission, withdrawal and authorised retry through its central transition helper. `packages/business-rules/src/approval-states.ts` defines the state graph. Gas price, flat fee, locations, quantity rules, product/service pricing, deposits, wallet/financial policies and staff access use the same definitions, requests, decisions, version history, activation and audit capability. Domain handlers validate/project values; they do not decide approvals.

Canonical states are DRAFT, PENDING_APPROVAL, CHANGES_REQUESTED, PARTIALLY_APPROVED, APPROVED, SCHEDULED, APPLYING, ACTIVE, REJECTED, CANCELLED, EXPIRED, FAILED, SUPERSEDED and ROLLED_BACK. Existing lowercase `status` values are retained in storage and legacy API fields to preserve compatibility; the `state` field and transition audit details expose uppercase canonical names. This is one graph with a casing adapter, not separate meanings or engines. Every public mutation has a strict schema; callers cannot set status, requester, approver or policy identity.

A draft preserves the active value. Submission checks current permission, value and base version and snapshots the effective policy. Decisions are append-only and tied to a revision. Material edits invalidate previous decisions by advancing the revision, then require resubmission. Independent distinct people satisfy dual approval; restricted requesters and privilege beneficiaries cannot approve themselves. Current role, active account, branch, delegation, qualifying safety authority and required verification evidence are rechecked. Stronger existing policy wins. Security approval-policy weakening remains unavailable in this release.

All approvals complete before activation begins. Future changes are scheduled in Africa/Lagos and stored with unambiguous timestamps. The worker and explicit activation path use the same implementation. A request/setting lock and unique version reference prevent duplicate effects. Activation rechecks current setting version, policy snapshot, requester and approvers. Projection and setting writes are protected by a savepoint; failures preserve the live version and persist FAILED, reason, attempts and notifications. Retry requires an authorised actor, sensitive verification and fresh revalidation. Stale approved requests become SUPERSEDED. Rollback is a new approved version linked to the reversed version; its old active request becomes historical ROLLED_BACK.

Transitions notify the requester and append audit events with previous/new state, actor, request, reason, revision and base version. Approver tasks are in-app; external messaging is not connected. The pre-existing verified, allowlisted emergency disable remains a protective exception for disabling delivery/payment integration, with its existing incident audit and owner notifications; it cannot enable a provider or bypass normal price approvals.

The Approval Centre offers summaries for eligible reviews, partial approvals, schedules, failures, pending and active requests, comparisons, review history, draft revision/submission, withdrawal and retry. Own/ineligible approvals are not active controls. Browser verification demonstrated the full draft lifecycle without changing the live price. Multi-connection production PostgreSQL, signed mobile/device distribution and independent production acceptance remain outstanding.
